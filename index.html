Help & dispatch guide

How the app works, common questions, and a practical path from your first call to getting paid.

How to use the app in 4 steps

  1. Find carriers: pick "Yesterday + today" and press Find carriers. Hot leads come first.
  2. Press ▶ Start calling: one carrier at a time with a big Call button.
  3. After each call, tap what happened: the app saves it and sets the next call for you.
  4. Every morning, open Today and press ▶ Start calling again for your follow-ups.

Common questions

Where does the carrier data come from?From public FMCSA records published on data.transportation.gov: the company census, Motus authority history, carrier authority status, insurance and BOC-3 filings.
How up to date is the data?Authority and status data update daily. Phone, owner and fleet details can lag one to three days for brand-new carriers.
Does it only show active carriers?Yes. Every carrier shown has active MC authority and an active USDOT number. Pending, inactive and revocation-pending carriers are left out.
Does it show owner names and phone numbers?Yes: the company officers listed on the carrier's MCS-150, plus the phone and email on file with FMCSA.
The Call button does nothing.On a computer, choose your calling app under "Call with" (Google Voice, Teams, Skype, Zoom Phone, or Just copy the number). Every Call click also copies the number.
Is DispatchWala affiliated with FMCSA?No. It's an independent tool that uses FMCSA's public open data.
Where are my saved leads and notes stored?Only in your own browser. Use Download backup under Scripts & settings to keep a copy.

From first call to getting paid

  1. Learn the basics first. Know the trailer types, how load boards work, what a broker does, and what a rate confirmation, BOL and POD are. Don't pay for a load board until you have a carrier.
  2. Find carriers to call. New authorities (last 7–30 days) are the warmest leads: they have trucks and no customers yet. Use Find carriers and filter by equipment you know how to book.
  3. Make the call. Ask for the owner by name, keep it short, and ask about equipment and lanes before you pitch. Use the script under Scripts & emails.
  4. Follow up. Most carriers sign on the 2nd to 5th touch. Log every call and set a follow-up; work your Today list daily.
  5. Sign them. Send a simple dispatch agreement (your %, what you do, how either side can stop). Move them to "Signed".
  6. Collect the carrier packet documents. MC authority letter, W-9, certificate of insurance, and factoring or bank details. Track them on the customer's checklist.
  7. Find a load. Search the load board for their equipment and lane. Check the broker's credit and days-to-pay before you call.
  8. Negotiate the rate. Know the lane's going rate per mile. Ask for more than you need, and never accept the first offer without countering.
  9. Get the rate confirmation. Check pickup and delivery times, addresses, rate, detention and lumper terms before the carrier signs it.
  10. Dispatch and track. Send the driver the load details, check in at pickup and delivery, and update the broker before they have to ask.
  11. Close it out. The signed BOL becomes the proof of delivery. Send POD and invoice to the broker or the factoring company.
  12. Get paid. Invoice the carrier your percentage once they're paid for the load.
Typical dispatch fee: most dispatchers charge 5–10% of the gross load pay. Box trucks and hotshots often pay a flat weekly fee instead. Charge only when the carrier gets paid.

Handling common objections

"I already have a dispatcher."Ask how it's going and what they'd change. Leave your number and set a follow-up for 30 days; many dispatcher relationships don't last.
"I find my own loads."Respect it. Ask how many hours a week that takes, and offer to book just one load so they can compare.
"Your fee is too high."Talk in dollars: if you get them $300 more per load and your fee is $200, they're ahead. Offer a trial load at your normal rate.
"I'm not ready yet / no insurance yet."New authorities often wait on insurance. Ask when it's expected and set the follow-up for that week.
"Send me an email."Say yes, send it within the hour, and ask for a good time to call back. Use the intro email template.

Daily routine that works

  • Start with your Today list: overdue follow-ups first, then due today. Call only between 8 AM and 9 PM in the carrier's local time.
  • Block 2 hours for new calls. Aim for 40–60 dials; log every outcome.
  • Move carriers to the next stage in My leads as soon as something changes.
  • End the day by setting follow-ups for everyone you talked to.
